The chapter in one sentence

Language modeling evolved from task-specific approaches into large, general-purpose foundation models, which shifted a major part of the challenge from “train the model” to “engineer the application around the model.”

3. Self-supervision → scaling

Self-supervised learning creates the learning signal from the data itself rather than requiring humans to manually label every example.

Massive raw data
→

→
Scale
  • Manual labels become less of a bottleneck.
  • Internet-scale corpora become useful training material.
  • More data + more compute + larger models can be exploited together.
My mental model: self-supervision did not create scaling by itself, but it removed a huge labeling constraint and made large-scale training practical.

AI Engineering

When teams no longer need to train a model from scratch for every task, engineering effort moves toward selecting models, creating context, integrating data and tools, evaluating behavior, managing cost and latency, and operating the system in production.

Key shift: the model becomes one component of the product rather than the entire product.

1. What is the simplest difference between masked and autoregressive language modeling?

Masked models reconstruct hidden information; autoregressive models predict the next token from prior context.

2. Why is self-supervision so important to scale?

Because the training signal can be derived from raw data itself, reducing dependence on manually labeled examples and allowing enormous datasets to be used.

3. Is every foundation model an LLM?

No. Foundation model is broader. Many LLMs are foundation models, but foundation models can also be vision, audio, or multimodal models.

4. Why did foundation models create a need for AI engineering?

Because broadly capable pretrained models shifted much of the work toward building, evaluating, integrating, and operating applications around the model.

5. What are the three layers of the AI engineering stack?

Application development, model development, and infrastructure.

6. How is evaluation different in AI engineering?

It often has to evaluate open-ended application behavior across quality, relevance, factuality, safety, cost, latency, and task success—not just a single model metric.

7. What is inference?

Using a trained model at run time to produce a prediction or generated response for an input.
